Sentry — What We’re Building

A short brief for partners · September 2026

1. Introduction

Sentry is a private company knowledge assistant. Think of it as a watcher over how your company actually works — policies, standards, past decisions — so people can ask the company instead of hunting through Slack, drives, or someone’s memory.

Generic AI tools are helpful, but they invent facts. Spreadsheets and wikis go stale. Sentry is built around a simple rule: cite what you know, or say you don’t know.

2. The problem we solve

  • Company knowledge lives in people’s heads and old chat threads.
  • Every new project restarts the same explanations.
  • New hires relearn “how we do things here.”
  • Related projects share a lot (N) and differ a little (K) — but teams re-brief everything anyway.

3. What Sentry does

Sentry gives your team a few connected surfaces on one grounded memory:

  • Chat — ask questions grounded in your uploaded documents.
  • Library / Dojo — train the company brain with policies, SOPs, exports.
  • Agents — named helpers (@Moony, @Ledger, @Quill…) that propose work inside your standards. People still approve.
  • Desktop home — a simple day view: chats, meetings, what needs you.
  • Asks — collaborative work items with shared ownership.
  • Knowledge pills — distill one chat into a reusable capsule for the next project.

4. Knowledge pills (the key idea)

When two projects are mostly the same, you shouldn’t start from zero.

N = what they share (goals, stack, decisions, constraints)
K = what’s different this time
Pill = a compressed version of N you can attach to a new chat

Example: Project A and Project B share onboarding flow, tech stack, and compliance rules (N). B only changes pricing and region (K). Make a pill from A, attach it to B’s chat, then only explain K.

  1. Open a chat that already has history → Make pill
  2. Open the new project chat → Attach pill
  3. Ask questions — answers take the pill into account

5. Who it’s for

Teams of about 5–50 people (agencies, professional services, light ops) where “how we work” still lives in a few people’s heads — and similar projects keep burning the same ramp-up time.

6. Trust rules

  1. Cite or refuse — no invented company facts.
  2. Chat is shared; Sidekick is personal — clear walls.
  3. Agents propose; people approve — no silent write-backs.
  4. Workspace isolation — every query stays in its tenant.

Remember: N + K → ready chat. The pill holds N. You only teach K. The model doesn’t need the whole old transcript again.
